I finally found something that satisfies my picky demands: a Russian-language [course](https://youtu.be/FyZFK4LBjj0) from Gosha Dudar's YouTube channel.
 
The course is mostly about the Spring framework. Yes, it's six years old now, and some of the information is outdated, but with Claude's help I filled in what had changed or wasn't covered. The author explains everything cleanly, with no filler and no wandering off into pointless debates. Huge thanks to him for that.
 
My stack was:
 
- Spring
- HTML
- Bootstrap for CSS (the author didn't want to bother with CSS)
- Docker + MySQL

The course covers how to create and handle requests, using a blog system as the example. Each post had a title, body text, a view counter, and "More", "Edit" and "Delete" buttons.
 
Things that used to feel like pure magic to me stopped feeling that way, at least a little. Before this, I didn't understand backend at all.
 
I can't say the course requires deep Java knowledge. Some of the necessary concepts are explained along the way, but someone with no OOP experience would definitely get lost. Luckily, that's not me. I honestly don't know why I was messing around with Go before, when I could have just used Java.
 
What genuinely surprised me was fragments: you can take a piece of HTML and insert it into another HTML page. It's really convenient. The code stays cleaner, and it's useful going forward. For example, a header with all its links is written once instead of on every page. It's a joy to work with.
